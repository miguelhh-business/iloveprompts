About

Prompts that actually work — built by someone who uses them daily.

iloveprompts.org is a small, independent project. It exists because writing good AI prompts is a skill, and most people shouldn't have to spend weeks learning frameworks like CO-STAR just to get a decent output from ChatGPT or Midjourney. These tools do that work for you, for free, without signup.

Why this site exists

When AI image and text generators went mainstream, a gap opened between two groups of users. On one side, power users — prompt engineers, designers, marketers — figured out structured frameworks (CO-STAR, parameter stacking, negative prompting, style tags) and got consistently excellent output. On the other side, everyone else typed a sentence, got a generic result, and walked away disappointed.

The frameworks weren't secret. They were just scattered across Reddit threads, X posts, YouTube tutorials, and Discord servers. And even once you knew them, you had to remember the exact structure every single time.

iloveprompts.org is my attempt to close that gap. Each tool on the site takes a proven prompt framework and wraps it in a simple guided form. You fill in the fields. The tool assembles the prompt in the correct structure for the specific AI platform you're targeting. You paste it. You get dramatically better results.

Design principle: No signup. No paywall. No "upgrade for more." Every tool is fully free and always will be. The site is supported by unobtrusive display ads.

What makes our prompts different

Framework-first, not template-first

Most prompt sites hand you a library of pre-written prompts: "100 ChatGPT prompts for marketers", "500 Midjourney prompts for logos", and so on. These are useful for inspiration, but they break the moment your use case doesn't match. Our tools use frameworks — structured patterns that work across use cases — so the output fits your situation, not a hypothetical one.

Platform-specific output

A great Midjourney prompt is not a great DALL·E prompt. A Claude prompt is not a ChatGPT prompt. Our tools know the syntax conventions of each platform and format the output accordingly — aspect ratio flags and style references for Midjourney, system-role framing for Claude, natural-language scene descriptions for Runway, and so on.

Three variations, every time

Every generator produces three versions of your prompt — not one. This is deliberate: AI output is probabilistic, and a single prompt often misses in subtle ways. Having three variations to test against each other is how professional prompt engineers actually work.

How the tools are kept current

AI platforms evolve quickly. Midjourney ships new parameters. Claude and ChatGPT release new models. Suno adjusts its style-tag syntax. I update the tools when those changes land, starting with the platforms I use most often and rolling out to the rest as user feedback comes in. If you notice an outdated prompt structure or a parameter that no longer works, please let me know — the contact page is the fastest way to reach me.
